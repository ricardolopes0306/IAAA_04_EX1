RULE 01

Compact E–W volume, closed S/E, open N/NW

Form: a compact two-storey mass with a protected interior patio; keep south/east openings shallow and direct living terraces / cross-ventilation toward the NW summer wind.

Why: combine the historic-centre compactness with the documented summer wind and the need to control solar exposure.

RULE 02

Thermal mass + deep horizontal shade

Form: high-inertia envelope, deep eaves/courtyard shade, no unshaded west glazing, winter-protected roof and water lines.

Why: respond to 40–45 °C tolerance conditions while retaining low winter sun and enabling night-flush cooling.

Climate-to-form statement: the courtyard is not an ornamental void; it is the mediator between thermal mass, shade, winter solar access and summer night ventilation.
BOARD 3 · CULTURE

Vernacular rules → two works → one trend

Culture is treated as a set of transferable architectural operations, not a style catalogue.

P4 · CULTURE
RULE 01

Local, low-energy material

Earth, stone, lime, timber and clay tile are the material family identified in the source deck. The proposal should retain the short-distance / repair logic where feasible.

RULE 02

Climate before equipment

Thick wall, whitewash, small/high openings, patio and adjustable shading: use mass, lime and air before adding mechanical dependence.

RULE 03

Passive, reversible, repairable

Prefer dry or lime-based assemblies and layers that can be repaired without locking the historic fabric into irreversible concrete construction.

ONE TREND

Earth: vernacular → contemporary

The source deck identifies a contemporary return to earth construction through compressed stabilised earth blocks, rammed earth with minimal cement, prefab panels and hybrid earth–timber systems.

Trend reading: earth is presented as a performance and low-carbon material system, not simply as a heritage image.

Trend credits in source deck: ArchDaily / plâno; Jornal de Notícias (2021); New Civil Engineer (2021).

Culture translated into the project

  • Keep the street-facing white/ochre register and party-wall scale.
  • Recover the patio as passive climate infrastructure.
  • Use lime, earth and timber as repairable layers where the existing construction allows.
  • Take the Malagueira lesson of an organising backbone, and the Jardim Diana lesson of one precise move.
BOARD 4 · SYNTHESIS

SWOT → two massing options → programme → declaration → validation

A decision board: every move is traceable to the territory, climate and culture boards.

P4 · SYNTHESIS

STRENGTHS

  • Living historic-centre context
  • Services on foot
  • Proven courtyard / party-wall typology
  • Csa climate offers passive-cooling potential

WEAKNESSES

  • Seismic constraint
  • Damp / rising moisture sensitivity
  • Extreme west heat
  • Plot-specific survey still incomplete

OPPORTUNITIES

  • Repair and reuse existing fabric
  • Patio as climate core
  • Earth/lime/timber compatibility
  • Housing + studio/workshop

THREATS

  • Over-sealing the courtyard
  • Irreversible concrete reinforcement
  • Uncontrolled west glazing / overheating
  • Heritage conflict if scale/openings drift

DECLARATION

“A compact Évora house that keeps the street memory, turns the courtyard into climate infrastructure, and adds only reversible layers of earth, lime and timber.”

The declaration condenses the documented site, climate and vernacular rules into one project position; it is a design statement, not a source quotation.

The four boards read as one chain: place defines constraints → climate defines massing → culture defines the architectural language and construction logic → synthesis turns those rules into a testable project.
